**Korea's market switches are now two — the *won level* and *semiconductor valuation* — and both are set from outside.**
① **The won is the ceiling constraint.** With USD/KRW near ~1,500–1,510 (off the ~1,530 weakest-since-2009 extreme but still elevated), foreign
capital outflows, import prices, and rate downside make it the *binding variable* on the Bank of Korea's room to
ease. The first channel by which the US Fed path transmits into Korea right now is not bonds but **the exchange
rate** — soft US jobs briefly firmed the won, but a firming dollar (DXY) and persistent foreign equity net-selling
keep pulling it back. A **re-accelerated third external force: oil/Middle-East geopolitics** — after a Jul-9
deflation, the weekend of 2026-07-11–12 brought fresh US–Iran strikes and an IRGC Hormuz-closure declaration,
gapping oil ~+3.4% (WTI ~$73.83) back up; a sustained Strait-of-Hormuz crude spike re-enters through import
prices and the won (Scout's English `finance` leads the geopolitics).
② **Index direction is held by semiconductor valuation.** The KOSPI is extremely concentrated in Samsung
Electronics, SK Hynix, and SK Square (most of its +163% YTD is chips/AI), so the global "AI valuation vs demand"
debate transmits **more forcefully to the KOSPI than to any other market**. Read Scout's (English `finance`)
dominant frame — "the Fed/front end is the switch, whether AI is inflationary or disinflationary is the direction
axis, the chip selloff is valuation not a demand break" — in a Korea key: **the global front-end repricing comes in
through the won, and the AI-valuation debate is amplified into the index through the chip concentration.** Do not
hand the downstream reader a conclusion — give where these two switches now point, what would break the frame, and
what to watch next.

**Falsifier (v2, 2026-07-08 — supersedes the old "index moves >±1.5% while USD/KRW quiet ±5 won" test, which was
snapshot-dependent and tested the *thesis* not its refutation).** Two switch-specific tests, on
*contemporaneous same-clock* snapshots (KOSPI onshore 15:30 close paired with the won's onshore 15:30 fixing; never
equity-close vs a later 24h FX print):
- **Semiconductor-switch test:** only on days the KOSPI net move exceeds ±2% (else NA). Measure the semi
  point-contribution = Σ(index weight × %-move) for Samsung + SK Hynix + SK Square vs total index net points. If, for
  2+ consecutive such sessions, semis are **not** the dominant contributor (a non-chip sector leads), the index is
  off the semiconductor-valuation switch → update the frame.
- **Won-switch test:** for 2+ consecutive sessions, USD/KRW moves >±10 won while the broad dollar (DXY) is ~flat
  (±0.3%). Secondary control: CNH/USD — the won often tracks the yuan on Asia-EM flows, so a >10-won move with DXY
  flat **but CNH also moving** is not cleanly domestic. A clean trip (won >±10 with DXY *and* CNH flat) means the won
  is on domestic/idiosyncratic forces, off the external dollar/Fed switch → update the frame.
